# Literary attribution and rights policy

## Inspiration

The Library of Babel is an independent exploration of an enormous combinatorial
library. Steven L. Peck’s _A Short Stay in Hell_ is a literary inspiration for the
opposing galleries and repeated facilities. See the [official publisher page](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/486146/a-short-stay-in-hell-by-peck-steven-l/9781807841348).
The reference identifies the work; it is not an endorsement or permission statement.
Neither Peck nor the publisher is affiliated with this project.

The README records implemented dimensions as project specifications rather than
asserting an exact transcription of the novella. No narrative passages, cover art,
author photograph or character dialogue are intentionally included as project
content. User-entered/generated book text is a separate matter; combinatorial
availability is not a license to reproduce or distribute someone else’s writing.

## Decisions in this revision

- Use The Library of Babel consistently as the project title.
- Replace author-name branding and the novella title as the opening-screen tagline
  with an independent project identity; retain a factual inspiration credit.
- Replace the named-character search example and autobiographical-search prompts
  with original, neutral exploration text. The game has no adaptation of the plot,
  characters or an afterlife quest.
- Link to the publisher instead of an unofficial full-text upload.
- Include no literary quotations. References alone do not authorize copying.
- Keep third-party software notices separate from the project's MIT license.

These changes reduce avoidable risks; they are not a legal determination that the
entire existing scene is non-infringing. A detailed recreation can raise adaptation
issues even without verbatim text, and a disclaimer does not cure infringement.
Before marketing the game as a faithful adaptation, adding recognizable narrative
material or claiming official status, obtain appropriate rights advice/permission.
No publisher or author has been contacted or clearance obtained by this revision.

## Why a name credit differs from a quotation

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that names, titles and ideas are not themselves
protected by copyright, while their original expression can be. A factual credit
using an author's name does not, by itself, copy the literary work. That does not
resolve separate trademark, endorsement, publicity or other jurisdiction-specific
questions. Avoid presenting the author's name as the game's creator or sponsor.
[U.S. Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html)

There is no fixed permissible word count for quotations under U.S. fair use;
context matters. A citation is not a substitute for permission or an applicable
exception. This project therefore does not rely on a “short quote is always safe”
rule. [U.S. Copyright Office fair-use FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html)

The Australian Copyright Council likewise distinguishes ideas/information from
their expression. U.S. fair use should not be assumed to apply worldwide.
[Ideas: Legal Protection](https://www.copyright.org.au/browse/book/ACC-Ideas%3A-Legal-Protection-INFO015)
The right to authorize adaptations is another relevant distinction; reducing
verbatim copying alone does not settle it.
[U.S. Copyright Act, chapter 1](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html)
